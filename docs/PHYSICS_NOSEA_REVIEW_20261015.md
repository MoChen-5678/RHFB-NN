# No-sea pairing prescription: independent physics review (2026-10-15)

## Decision

There is not enough evidence to name one of the current whole-space or projected densities as the uniquely correct physical no-sea prescription for this calculation. The papers use related but non-identical conventions, and the current code has not shown their equivalence for the PKA1+D1S odd system.

Three claims should therefore be withdrawn pending a derivation/reference comparison:

1. `P+ ρ P+`, `P+ κ P+ᵀ` is not the same finite-field HFB/RHFB stationary state as the same-field full-BdG solution when cross-sector pairing is present. It is a separately projected model. The saved O17 comparison already falsifies exact equivalence for those fields.
2. A full primitive-space pure generalized density and a small `[Hμ,R]` residual do not, by themselves, certify the intended no-sea vacuum. They establish algebraic stationarity for the supplied full-space Hamiltonian and state. The branch/field-operator convention remains an additional physical input.
3. Neither `W=-M` nor a numerical cutoff on “sea content” of the final Bogoliubov density is established as a universal physical axiom. `W=-M` has a free-Dirac-gap motivation; the density cutoff has a large-gap/neglect motivation. Neither source supplies a universal finite-field threshold or an error bound for this O17 calculation.

For a PKA1+D1S RHFB reproduction, the next reference should first reproduce the no-sea field-operator convention and DWS expansion described by Geng and Long (2022) and Long et al. (2008), then document its exact map into the project's full primitive/Nambu variables. DIRHB's Eqs. (46)–(48) should be treated as a distinct, explicit RHB branch prescription and compared as a separate convention. This recommendation does not prove that either published even-system convention extends unchanged to odd O17 blocking.

## Evidence ledger

Each item separates what the source says, what follows mathematically, what the current workspace records, and what remains unproved.

### 1. Negative-energy Dirac basis states are not the same label as occupied sea quasiparticle branches

**Source statement.** Long et al. define the no-sea Hartree-Fock reference by omitting negative-energy single-particle contributions from the energy functional (Eq. (7), PDF p. 2). They then solve the coupled RHFB equations (Eqs. (19)–(21), PDF pp. 4–5), and expand both U and V components on positive- and negative-energy DWS basis functions (Eqs. (31)–(34), PDF pp. 5–6). Geng and Long make the distinction especially explicit: both positive- and negative-energy spherical Dirac solutions are retained in the U,V expansion for completeness, while the no-sea approximation means neglecting the Dirac sea in the densities/currents (Eq. (23) and the paragraph immediately following, PDF pp. 9–10). For PKA1, they find that additional negative-energy DWS expansion states materially improve convergence in 156Sm (PDF pp. 9–11). [Long et al., arXiv:0812.1103](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0812.1103); [Geng & Long, arXiv:2202.00482](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.00482).

**Mathematical inference.** A coefficient on a negative-energy *basis function* can be required to represent a complete quasiparticle spinor without asserting occupation of the Dirac sea or adding that state as an independent occupied nucleon. Conversely, projecting every final U,V/density component onto a normal positive-energy subspace is an additional approximation/constraint; basis completeness alone neither requires nor justifies that projection. In a no-sea field-operator formulation the physical density is determined by the allowed operators and reference vacuum, not by the sign of every coordinate used to expand a spinor.

**Current program evidence.** [`selfconsistent_stationarity.py`](../dpl_rhf/rhfb/selfconsistent_stationarity.py#L1) explicitly says it “does not select or certify a no-sea branch.” It rebuilds the full normal field and full D1S action and evaluates the full-space commutator (lines 57–69, 114–165). [`bdg_branch_filter.py`](../dpl_rhf/rhfb/bdg_branch_filter.py#L1) instead encodes a caller-supplied finite BdG band certificate; the code itself says its booleans are audit gates and do not prove caller assertions (lines 29–35, 56–71). Neither implementation currently derives the cited paper's no-sea field-operator convention from the project's primitive-space state.

**Not established.** The published basis-expansion statement does not, on its own, prove that the project's unconstrained full primitive-space generalized-density variation is equivalent to the RHFB functional/quantization used in those papers. The exact map of the DWS basis, HF particle space, U,V expansion, density, pairing tensor, and pairing interaction still needs to be derived. The cited applications are even systems and do not certify the odd blocked extension.

### 2. DIRHB uses a different explicit RHB quasiparticle-vacuum rule

**Source statement.** Nikšić et al. explain that an RHB eigenvector `(U,V)` at `E>0` has particle-hole partner `(V*,U*)` at `-E` (discussion after Eq. (41), PDF p. 11). They distinguish particle-labelled modes above the Dirac sea from sea-labelled modes using the large gap, then define the no-sea vacuum by choosing particle annihilators with `Eₚ>0` and Dirac-sea annihilators with `Eₐ<0` (Eq. (46), PDF p. 12). Both selected sums appear in the normal density and pairing tensor (Eqs. (47)–(48), PDF pp. 12–13). The authors say the sea-labelled `Vₙₐ` coefficients are negligible due to the large gap and are often omitted; they also state that this makes κ only approximately antisymmetric (PDF p. 13). DIRHB is explicitly an even-even, time-reversal-symmetric implementation (program summary, PDF p. 1). [Nikšić et al., arXiv:1403.4039](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1403.4039).

**Mathematical inference.** Those equations prescribe a mixed sign choice over physically labelled quasiparticle branches. They do not prescribe the normal-Dirac double projection `P+ρP+`, `P+κP+ᵀ`. Dropping the sea-labelled terms is an approximation stated by the authors, not an exact identity. Their “negligible” statement provides no universal norm threshold, observable error bound, or guarantee for a different finite basis, functional, or odd blocked state.

**Current program evidence.** The saved O17 ledger compares a same-frozen-field whole BdG projector with strict normal-P projection. For neutrons, the whole-state full-H commutator is `1.00e-10 MeV`, while the projected state gives `5.00e-3 MeV` against that same full H (its own projected-H commutator is `3.07e-11 MeV`); for protons the corresponding values are `9.90e-11`, `5.52e-3`, and `2.95e-11 MeV`. The anomalous-density difference is `6.64e-5` (n) and `3.89e-4` (p); these are small in this record but nonzero. This is a frozen-field, conditional G/F reference, not a physical branch certification or self-consistent O17 result. The ledger labels the entire physical solver unaccepted. [ROOT_PHYSICS_REVIEW_LEDGER.json](../outputs/validation/O17_full_fields_physics_20261006/ROOT_PHYSICS_REVIEW_LEDGER.json).

**Not established.** The ledger does not contain a complete, physically labelled particle/sea BdG band enclosure or prove that its conditional G/F normal split is the DIRHB physical branch. The saved BdG minimum `|E|` is not a particle/sea band edge. The DIRHB even-even prescription does not resolve how the intended odd O17 blocked orbit should be initialized and tracked.

### 3. A scalar BdG window filter is a finite-matrix encoding, not a branch ontology

**Source statement.** DIRHB motivates branch recognition by the large separation between the Fermi and Dirac seas (about 1200 MeV in its shifted convention) and states the signed choices in Eq. (46); it does not define a scalar `Λ` filter (PDF pp. 11–13). [Nikšić et al., arXiv:1403.4039](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1403.4039).

**Mathematical inference.** For a finite Hermitian particle-hole-symmetric BdG matrix, the exact filter

`RΛ = 1/2 [I − sgn(Hμ) sgn(Λ² I − Hμ²)]`

encodes that mixed sign rule only after physical particle and sea annihilator bands have already been assigned and the *complete* finite bands satisfy `max |Eₚ| < Λ < min |Eₐ|`, with gaps at `0` and `±Λ`. It can retain cross-sector κ because it acts on the full Hμ. If a finite cutoff cuts a particle continuum, a branch is misclassified. A matrix split or a formal passing certificate cannot supply the missing physical labels.

**Current program evidence.** `BandEnclosureCertificate.validate` enforces the finite-window inequality and complete-band flags in [`bdg_branch_filter.py`](../dpl_rhf/rhfb/bdg_branch_filter.py#L29); `mixed_sign_bdg_projector` checks Hermiticity and particle-hole symmetry and reports numerical approximation diagnostics (lines 157 onward). This is appropriately documented as a finite-space diagnostic. The O17 ledger has zero BdG eigensolver calls and no complete branch-labelled BdG band bounds, so it does not satisfy this certificate for O17.

**Not established.** No stored evidence proves that a single `Λ` separates the complete particle and sea bands in the O17 Nambu space, much less in a continuum/basis-limit calculation. If the cluster is not isolated, Riesz/Kato continuation also cannot transport it through a selected/unselected crossing or continuum contact.

### 4. `W=-M` is motivated by the free spectrum, not proved as an interacting universal cutoff

**Source statement.** DIRHB notes that for `h_D−m`, the positive continuum begins at zero and the negative continuum at `−2m`, with a large intervening gap (PDF p. 12). This makes `W=-M` the natural midpoint of the *free shifted* gap. The 2008 and 2022 RHFB papers instead define their no-sea reference through the HF field/operator and DWS construction; the 2022 paper uses explicit positive/negative DWS expansion cutoffs and convergence checks, not a universal interacting `W=-M` rule (PDF pp. 9–11). [DIRHB](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1403.4039); [Geng & Long](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.00482); [Long et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0812.1103).

**Mathematical inference.** In the free rest-mass-subtracted Dirac operator, `-M` lies halfway between the continua. For an interacting finite matrix, its use as a separator needs a field- and basis-specific gap/inertia proof and a physical identification of the two sides. A midpoint inherited from the free operator is a useful predeclared candidate, not a theorem that every PKA1 field, truncation, and convention has the same inertia or margin. Also, this normal-H `W` is a different object from the BdG magnitude divider `Λ`.

**Current program evidence.** The old theory note calls `W=-M` a production constraint, but the later frozen-field implementation itself calls it the “unchanged historical” policy and labels its margins “diagnostic-selected fail-closed engineering margins,” not a cited universal cutoff ([`fixed_field_dirac_branch_reference.py`](../dpl_rhf/rhfb/fixed_field_dirac_branch_reference.py#L240), [lines 262–276](../dpl_rhf/rhfb/fixed_field_dirac_branch_reference.py#L262)). The saved O17 audit says the historical `W=-M` gate was refused at 198/234 counts and its predeclared margins were unpassed; a separate G/F-derived finite-matrix split gave 216/216 inertia but explicitly did not establish physical Dirac labels ([O17 audit](O17_BRANCH_EVIDENCE_AUDIT_20261007.md), [ledger](../outputs/validation/O17_full_fields_physics_20261006/ROOT_PHYSICS_REVIEW_LEDGER.json)).

**Not established.** The old `NO_REFRESH_THEORY_AND_WORKFLOW.md` claim that the interacting `W=-M` inequality “simultaneously excludes the Dirac sea” is not established for the saved O17 fields by the cited free-gap argument. The alternative G/F split is not a replacement physical prescription either. No result here proves that `W=-M` fails for all fields or that a physical separator does not exist.

## Is a numerical “sea-content” threshold defensible?

Not as a physical acceptance criterion without a derivation. Three quantities must not be conflated: (a) expansion weight on negative-energy DWS basis functions, (b) density/pairing contributions from the DIRHB sea-labelled quasiparticle branch, and (c) coupling of the full pairing field between normal particle and sea-labelled sectors. The 2022 PKA1 calculations show that (a) can be necessary for convergence even when the no-sea density convention omits Dirac-sea contributions. DIRHB says (b) is often negligible but does not give a fixed tolerance and warns that omission alters exact κ antisymmetry. A small value of one quantity does not bound the other two.

If the selected published convention permits omitting a sea-labelled density/pairing term, report that term and its effect as a controlled approximation: compare density, energy, full residual, κ antisymmetry, particle number, and the observables used for acceptance under increasing basis/cutoff and with/without the term in an independently branch-labelled reference. Set tolerances from the project's stated numerical/observable accuracy. Do not accept a state because a hand-chosen sea fraction is below a percentage, and do not use such a threshold to select the branch during optimization.

## Executable next reference, without assuming the answer

1. **Freeze the target convention in writing.** For a D-RHFB reproduction, start from the RHFB field-operator/no-sea definition in Long et al. and Geng–Long: state which single-particle orbitals define the HF particle space, which negative-energy DWS modes are retained only for U,V expansion completeness, and how ρ and κ are assembled. Separately state the alternative DIRHB Eq. (46) mixed quasiparticle-branch rule. Do not call them equivalent until their finite-space equations and energy functional have been mapped term by term.
2. **Build one small fixed-field derivation/reference.** Use complete positive/negative normal Dirac basis sectors, a nonzero full D1S Δ including particle–sea matrix elements, and a declared even vacuum. Independently form (i) the branch-explicit DIRHB density and (ii) the RHFB no-sea density from the chosen HF field-operator convention. Compare ρ, κ, energy, κ antisymmetry, purity, and every block of `[Hμ,R]`. This directly tests the disputed mapping; a normal-P double projection should be included only as a third, explicitly constrained model.
3. **Then add odd blocking as its own gate.** Start from the validated even reference, perform the declared particle/hole quasiparticle occupation swap, and verify number parity and blocked-state identity through any continuation. A parity flag alone is insufficient. The cited DIRHB calculations are even-even; the odd extension remains a project-specific result.
4. **Certify finite branch labels before using an eigen-free filter.** For each frozen field, record complete dimension/rank accounting and branch labels from the independent reference, or a physically initialized whole-cluster projector with a maintained gap along a declared homotopy. Only then test the full-BdG `Λ` filter or a resolvent continuation. Fail the physical certificate if the branch gap closes; do not replace it by the G/F mathematical split or a learned density threshold.
5. **Only after these gates pass, couple the fields self-consistently.** Retain the selected convention's complete normal and pairing contributions, including rearrangement and time-odd fields required for odd O17. The current self-consistent stationarity loss is an algebraic prototype, not this accepted physical solver.

This sequence can establish a convention-specific finite-space reference. It cannot prove equivalence for all fields or basis limits in advance. The correct outcome may be that the DIRHB mixed-sign scalar filter is unavailable for a given finite window, or that the full unconstrained primitive-space stationarity problem is not the same target as the published no-sea RHFB model.
