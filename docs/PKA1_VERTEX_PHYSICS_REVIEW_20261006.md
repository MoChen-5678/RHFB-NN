# PKA1 derivative vertices and contact terms: literature review

**Scope.** This is a literature-to-functional review of the current Cartesian RHFB energy prototype. It changes no production code and certifies no numerical convergence. Algebraic closure shows that a derivative matches the implemented scalar; it does not prove that the scalar is the intended physical functional.

## Sources and their scope

Peng, Geng, and Long, *Chinese Physics C* **49**, 074107 (2025), Sec. II.A, Eqs. (1)–(8), give the RHF two-body energy, the rho-T and symmetrized rho-VT vertices, and the pion-PV vertex. The article states that density-dependent couplings generate rearrangement and uses a static no-retardation Yukawa propagator. It discusses PKO1–3 and PKA1, but its numerical calculations use PKO interactions, so it is not a PKA1-specific validation of this implementation. [Official CPC article](https://cpc.ihep.ac.cn/article/doi/10.1088/1674-1137/adc11e).

[Published PKA1 paper](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevC.76.034314), [arXiv record and manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/0706.3497). The published abstract does not establish a general complex 3D or anomalous-density/contact extension.

For the differential-operator convention and spherical contact reductions, the local RHF-e manuscript is more explicit: Eqs. (1.24)–(1.25), PDF p. 6, state that the derivative acts on the propagator; rho-T compensation and contact reductions are Eqs. ## Derivative vertices and the discrete adjoint

Let J(x) = psi-bar(x) Gamma psi(x), g(x) be a local density-dependent vertex, and D(x,y) = D(y,x) the static propagator. With RHF-e's convention that the derivative in the vertex acts on D, a derivative-endpoint factor is

$$\int dx\,dy\;g(x)J(x)\,\partial_{x_i}D(x,y)\,g(y)J'(y).$$

Integration by parts in x, with vanishing boundary flux, transfers the derivative to the entire product gJ. In a discretization with the physical quadrature metric this is the weighted adjoint G-dagger[gJ]. It therefore includes the discrete product-rule contribution from spatial variation of g, including its density dependence. Replacing it by g G-dagger[J] would generally omit that contribution. With derivatives at both ends the corresponding weak form is the contraction of G-dagger[gJ], D, and G-dagger[gJ']. The code's `GreenOperator.gradient_adjoint` is explicitly defined as this weighted adjoint, and the derivative routines pass the vertex-weighted current into it. This is the appropriate weak-form route for the stated vertex convention, conditional on discrete quadrature and boundary consistency.

CPC Eq. (4e) is an ordered sum, not one doubled vertex: V(x)D T(y) + T(x)D V(y), where T carries the derivative. The Fock functional must retain both orientations because the transition densities and endpoints are complex and ordered. The implementation's `derivative_on_left=False/True` paths represent these two placements separately. The Hartree term can be split equally between VT and TV labels because, for real local couplings and a symmetric static Green operator, swapping integration endpoints makes the scalar contractions equal. This supports the factors by algebra, but is not a PKA1 numerical comparison.

The same vertices fix static Lorentz bookkeeping. In the (+---) convention, all four rho-T index components mu = 0,1,2,3 remain and carry (+---) contraction signs; the derivative index is spatial because the static no-retardation reduction sets partial_0 D = 0. The mixed rho-VT term pairs each vector component j^mu with the tensor component carrying the same mu and metric sign. Pion-PV is static-spatial, with its overall minus sign from CPC Eq. (4f). The code's four-mu loops and metric signs for rho-T/mixed terms, and three spatial pion operators with their direct/Fock prefactors, appear consistent by inspection. This is not an independent gamma-matrix trace or a 3D-to-spherical oracle.

## Contact distributions and compensation terms

Two local-looking objects must be kept conceptually separate. Differentiating a finite-range Green kernel twice produces a coincident distributional/Hessian contribution. The spatial-operator documentation says the projected `derivative_x=True, derivative_y=True` kernel retains its coincident radial entry. That is an implementation intent; no independent test here proves that the discrete Hessian reproduces the continuum delta distribution with the right normalization.

RHF-e separately introduces compensation Hamiltonians H-delta: Eq. (3.204) for rho-T and Eq. (3.293) for pion-PV, each with coefficient 1/2 x 1/3 = 1/6. Its spherical reductions retain those terms alongside the derivative contact: rho-T Eqs. (3.202), (3.207), and combined result (3.352); pion-PV Eqs. (3.292), (3.294), and combined result (3.296). At the continuum operator level H-delta and the differentiated-propagator distribution are distinct contributions in this decomposition. The code visibly adds one H-delta expectation per channel and has no second separately named compensation insertion. That does not prove either a discrete over-count or under-count: the general 3D projected Green Hessian plus the single H-delta expectation has not been independently matched to the spherical formulas. Therefore this review cannot conclude “double compensation” or “no double counting” for the discretized functional.

The normal-density contact expression is a plausible direct-minus-exchange expectation of the cited compensation Hamiltonian. What remains unverified is that the present pointwise 3D spinor trace equals all RHF-e spherical reductions for arbitrary complex normal densities and unequal neutron/proton fields. The RHFB model used here assigns the particle-hole meson-exchange channel and particle-particle anomalous channel to D1S pairing, as in the hybrid prescription of Geng and Long, *Phys. Rev. C* **105**, 034329 (2022), Sec. II and Eqs. (20a)–(20d). The absence of a meson/contact anomalous contraction is therefore a model channel choice, not an inferred omission from Wick's theorem. The missing independent normal-contact 3D trace/kernel oracle remains a real physical-reference gap. [Geng and Long manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00482).

## Material model differences and unresolved points

1. **Density choice.** The O-RHF paper states that the fitted PKO and PKA1 couplings are functions of rho-b = psi-bar gamma-zero psi = j-zero (Sec. II.A, around Eq. (8)). This project has deliberately selected the invariant sqrt(j_mu j^mu) prescription, consistent with the covariant Fuchs construction; that authorized choice should be preserved. They agree for a static rest-frame state with spatial current zero, but differ for time-odd/current-carrying states. The implementation is therefore a covariant PKA1-style extension off the original fitted rest-frame prescription; successful rearrangement closure does not prove equivalence to the original fit in current-carrying states.

2. **Pion-PV Hartree in time-reversal-breaking states.** The current functional calculates `pion_pv_direct` from the static spatial axial-current source. The time-even, time-reversal-invariant reference solutions suppress this source because the summed spatial axial current is time-reversal odd. Parity alone does not force the local source to zero: a parity eigenstate may have a spatially varying odd pseudoscalar divergence. A blocked/current-carrying state can break time reversal and, under the same Lagrangian, permit a nonzero pion Hartree source. Thus the O-RHF description of pion-PV as Fock-only for its reference calculation does not establish that this general time-odd direct term is wrong. The production choice to include the direct term is consistent with the general vertex, but its independent matrix elements and symmetry behavior have not been compared with a PKA1 reference. No exclusion is justified by the sources reviewed here.

3. **Compensation.** The code adds each cited compensation Hamiltonian once, but exact matching to the continuum coincident Hessian/contact decomposition remains open. This is the main unresolved contact issue; an algebraic energy/gradient closure using the same kernel cannot settle it.

4. **Reference coverage.** The 2025 O-RHF article publishes the vertex structure and density prescription, but uses PKO interactions in its results and defers detailed energy formulas to prior work. Its Eq. (4) is structural evidence, not a PKA1-specific check of coupling conversions, compensation terms, or RHFB normal/anomalous channel assignment.

## Assessment

The G-dagger[gJ] weak form and both ordered VT/TV Fock orientations follow from the published vertices and the derivative-on-propagator convention. The static Lorentz-index pattern appears consistent by inspection. The continuum reference treats compensation Hamiltonians separately from the derivative-kernel distribution, but the current discretization has no independent test establishing their correct combined normalization.

No definite missing gradient-of-coupling term, VT/TV orientation omission, or Lorentz sign error is supported by this review. The significant open validations are the continuum-to-discrete contact identity and independent pion-PV direct-source behavior in time-reversal-breaking states. The invariant-current density choice is an intentional covariant extension, not a reproduction proven equivalent to the fitted j-zero prescription outside the static rest frame. Existing finite-difference, rearrangement, or time-reversal closure demonstrates internal consistency only; it cannot close these physical-reference gaps.
