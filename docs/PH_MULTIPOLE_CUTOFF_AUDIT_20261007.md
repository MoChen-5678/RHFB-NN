# Derivative-channel Green multipole cutoff audit

**Decision:** the current `Lmax=2K` default is justified as a *finite-band central-convolution closure* for a constant-coupling bilinear source, not as a proven cutoff for the full PKA1 derivative channels. For the latter, `Lmax=2K` is currently a chosen cutoff. Neither the papers nor the existing validation establishes a complete finite-κ derivative-kernel upper bound or angular convergence. Do not describe the O17 K=3, L=6 result as angularly converged.

## What the cited angular formulas establish

Geng and Long's D-RHFB paper [arXiv:2202.00482](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00482) gives the static meson propagator in Eq. (9), PDF p. 4, and derives the deformed RHFB equations in a spherical Dirac-Woods-Saxon expansion. It explicitly keeps both positive- and negative-energy basis states for expansion completeness (Sec. II.3; Sec. III.1, PDF pp. 10–12), and reports that π-PV/ρ-T couple larger-κ components and require additional negative-energy basis states for convergence (Sec. II.4 and III.1–III.2). Appendix A gives axial/reflection-symmetric multipole expansions of density-dependent couplings, e.g. Eq. (51), and the derivative-bearing ρ-T/ρ-VT structures. These formulas support the need to represent derivative channels and coupling expansions; they do not state that a propagator multipole cutoff of twice the primitive orbital cutoff is exact for a differentiated 3D kernel.

The RHF-e manuscript gives the static Yukawa multipole expansion in Eqs. (3.15)–(3.16), PDF p. 23, and writes the derivative placement explicitly: Eq. (1.25), PDF p. 6, puts spatial derivatives on the propagator for ρ-T, ρ-VT, and π-PV. Its channel reductions (including π-PV Eqs. (3.290)–(3.296), and ρ-T Eqs. (3.202)–(3.207)/(3.348)–(3.353)) establish angularly reduced expressions for the spherical formalism, not a general finite-K theorem for the present 3D discretization. The 2025 O-RHF article likewise presents the vertex structures, not a 3D derivative Green-cutoff proof. [Peng, Geng & Long, CPC 49 (2025) 074107](https://cpc.ihep.ac.cn/article/doi/10.1088/1674-1137/adc11e).

For an undifferentiated rotationally invariant central propagator and a constant coupling, a product of two spinor components with orbital angular momenta no larger than K has source multipoles no larger than 2K (Clebsch–Gordan triangle rule). The central convolution preserves each source multipole. This is the algebraic setting in which `2K` is a sufficient band limit. It does not transfer automatically to endpoint derivatives: a spatial derivative transforms as a rank-one spherical tensor and recouples angular channels; derivatives at both ends introduce further tensor coupling and a coincident Hessian/contact contribution. The exact admissible propagator-L bound depends on the precise projected operator, endpoint integration/adjoint, spin-angular recoupling, and whether the target is the kernel itself or only its matrix elements between the finite basis. The cited papers do not provide the theorem needed to identify one universal exact bound here, so this note does not assign one.

## Why density-dependent PKA1 vertices remove a simple bandlimit

The RHF-e convention differentiates the propagator, after which the discrete weighted-adjoint form acts on the full vertex-weighted source `g(r)J(r)` (see [the existing derivative-vertex review](PKA1_VERTEX_PHYSICS_REVIEW_20261006.md)). Thus even if the primitive bilinear J is band-limited, the product need not be. In the selected covariant prescription, vertices depend on the current invariant \(\sqrt{j_\mu j^\mu}\); taking square roots and the fitted nonlinear density functions of angularly varying currents does not preserve finite spherical-harmonic support in general. A non-polynomial angular function can carry arbitrarily high multipoles in the continuum. This is a mathematical consequence of the chosen functional, not a claim that every particular solution has large high-L weight. Special symmetry, a polynomial coupling with known degree, or a separately proven truncation could restore a finite bound, but none is available as a general PKA1 statement.

## Validation status and next claim that is supportable

The current `GreenOperator` chooses `lmax = 2 * basis.kappa_max` by default ([spatial.py](/home/ubuntu/rhf/DFT-RHF-NoRefresh/variants/blocked-rhfb/dpl_rhf/rhfb/spatial.py)); O17 K=3 therefore uses L=6. The existing Gaussian propagator reference checks only a bare, constant-coupling spherical l=0 source and explicitly excludes derivative/contact and angular-convergence claims ([reference note](GREEN_PROPAGATOR_GAUSSIAN_REFERENCE_20261006.md)). The derivative/current tests and O17 closure at the selected L can establish internal consistency at that cutoff, not completeness of the omitted L channels.

A justified next validation is a fixed-primitive-space L sweep of representative π-PV, ρ-T, V-T and T-V matrix elements/energy-gradient components, independently comparing at least one small-space derivative kernel against direct angular-coordinate integration or analytic vector-spherical-harmonic recoupling. Record the largest-L tail separately for constant and self-consistent density-dependent vertices. Also vary angular quadrature enough to avoid aliasing the higher-L source. These are convergence checks, not a reason to redefine the physical model or discard the derivative channels. Until such evidence exists, report `L=2K` as the configured cutoff and state that derivative-channel angular convergence remains open.
