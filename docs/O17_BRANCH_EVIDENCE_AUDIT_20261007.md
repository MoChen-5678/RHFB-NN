# O17 branch evidence audit (2026-10-07)

## Finding

The saved O17 independent-reference JSON does **not** contain complete particle- and sea-labelled BdG quasiparticle band bounds. It records chemical potentials, minimum quasiparticle gaps, continuation diagnostics, and full **normal-H** spectra, but not the complete BdG spectrum partitioned into Eq. (46) particle and sea annihilator branches. Therefore the saved record does not establish a strict single-divider certificate for the finite `D=432` Nambu problem. No new spectral calculation was made and no large arrays were diagonalized for this audit.

The conditional fixed-field reference records `μ_n = −2.8962002784 MeV` and `μ_p = −0.9994175255 MeV`, each described as the normal-HF target-number bracket midpoint, shared by the whole and projected branches. The JSON explicitly says these are not self-consistent NN chemical potentials. It also stores continuation minimum `|E_qp|` values (`0.0715439 MeV` neutron, `0.0121352 MeV` proton at full pairing) and checks particle-hole spectral pairing, but those values are not extrema of the two physically labelled bands and do not locate a gap between those bands. ## Exact evidence needed for a one-divider certificate

For the full finite Hermitian `H_μ` at one frozen field and one stated `μ`, a scalar `Λ` can encode the mixed DIRHB sign choice only if the **complete, branch-labelled** finite bands satisfy

`max_{p branch} |E_p| < Λ < min_{sea branch} |E_a|`,

with certified margins from zero and from `±Λ`, complete dimension/rank accounting, and the declared particle-hole partner convention. The stored normal-H list can help construct a zero-pairing reference, and the fixed `μ` is known for the frozen comparison, but the cited JSON does not provide the BdG band extrema or a validated physical assignment needed to certify the inequality. It is therefore not appropriate to fill the gap by reading selected entries out of an NPZ, treating the reported minimum qp gap as a band edge, or inferring a branch from the mathematical G/F split. This is an evidence limitation, not a proof that no such divider exists for `D=432`.

The physical prescription itself is branch-defined: DIRHB selects `E_p>0` for particle modes and `E_a<0` for Dirac-sea modes (Eq. 46), with both sectors in the density and pairing sums (Eqs. 47–48, PDF pp. 12–13). It uses the large relativistic gap to identify those modes; it does not define their identity by an arbitrary finite-space G/F inertia split. [Nikšić et al., arXiv:1403.4039](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1403.4039).

## Riesz/Kato continuation: conditional route and limits

For a differentiable finite-dimensional Hermitian path `H(t)`, a full selected subspace can be transported without eigenvectors if it is already physically identified at `t=0` and remains an isolated spectral cluster of constant rank for every `t` in the path. One fixed contour (or a continuously moved contour) must remain in the resolvent set and enclose exactly that cluster. Then

`P(t) = (1/(2πi)) ∮Γ (zI−H(t))⁻¹ dz`,

`Ṗ(t) = (1/(2πi)) ∮Γ (zI−H)⁻¹ Ḣ (zI−H)⁻¹ dz`.

These formulas use shifted linear solves and can be differentiated through a neural-field dependence on `H`; they do not require extracting eigenvectors or replacing/refreshing the NN primitive basis. A uniform contour distance supplies resolvent conditioning. Internal degeneracies within the selected cluster are allowed because the projector tracks the entire cluster, not an ordered basis. Kato's perturbation theory supplies the spectral-projection framework; the contour formula and gap-dependent smoothness are also laid out explicitly for Hermitian matrix families in [Fermanian-Kammerer et al., Sec. 3](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00220-021-04147-2), with Kato's original closed-operator perturbation treatment at [J-STAGE, 1952](https://doi.org/10.2969/jmsj/00430323).

The current O17 JSON does not certify the required uniform cluster gap along its five sampled pairing scales. Its minimum `|E_qp|` and selected-mode overlap are not substitutes for that cluster separation. A selected/unselected crossing, contact, change of rank, or entry into continuous spectrum destroys the isolating contour; at a degeneracy, only the complete degenerate eigenspace has a canonical projector. An individual blocked mode inside such a cluster needs an additional symmetry label or explicit diabatic transport, and an avoided crossing can exchange the physical character while the whole cluster remains smooth. If `H` depends on NN fields, the resolvent derivative contains the full `Ḣ` induced by those fields; it is useful as a branch-transport or residual auxiliary only while the contour/gap is certified. It cannot create physical labels from the interacting spectrum, prove that the starting vacuum is the intended no-sea vacuum, or establish a self-consistent RHFB minimum.

This audit supports neither a passed no-sea prescription nor a physical O17 result. For the saved finite reference, a future strict certificate would need a branch-labelled complete BdG band enclosure or a certified homotopy projector with uniform gap/rank and explicit blocked-state tracking, all tied to the exact saved `h`, full `Δ`, and `μ` hashes.
