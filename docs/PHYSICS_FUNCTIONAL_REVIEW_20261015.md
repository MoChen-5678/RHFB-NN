# Physics audit of the blocked Cartesian PKA1/RHFB functional

**Scope and verdict.** This is a source-to-implementation review, not a numerical validation. The code is a useful general-m, complex-spinor extension, but calling it a validated “full PKA1 time-odd RHFB model” goes beyond the cited papers. The PKA1 vertices and derivative channels are grounded in the published Lagrangian; the current-invariant density argument and unrestricted current-carrying evaluation are a definite off-fit extension. Full upper/lower-sector and all-J like-particle D1S pairing has direct precedent in the deformed RHFB paper. The Cartesian implementation’s arbitrary complex anomalous density and explicit exclusion of neutron-proton pairing are not independently benchmarked against that axial reference.

## Density argument and time-odd fields

The original PKA1 paper defines the fitted couplings as functions of baryonic density ρ_b and gives the PKA1 density-dependent forms in Eqs. (9)–(11) ([Long et al., PRC 76, 034314 (2007), arXiv:0706.3497](https://arxiv.org/abs/0706.3497), Sec. II, PDF pp. 3–4). The earlier DDRHF formulation that supplies those density-dependent forms explicitly defines ρ_b as the zeroth component of the nucleon current, \(j^0=\bar\psi\gamma^0\psi\) ([Long, Van Giai & Meng, arXiv:nucl-th/0608009](https://arxiv.org/abs/nucl-th/0608009), Sec. II.A, Eq. (8), PDF p. 5; see also [arXiv:nucl-th/0512086](https://arxiv.org/abs/nucl-th/0512086), Sec. II, Eqs. (4)–(6), PDF pp. 2–3). This establishes the published static DDRHF/PKA1 density convention. Those papers do not clearly specify whether this is also intended as the complete covariant prescription for arbitrary current-carrying states. The PKA1 fit uses bulk nuclear-matter properties, binding energies and charge radii, shell gaps and spin-orbit splittings (2007 paper, Sec. II, PDF p. 4; tables and discussion pp. 6–12). Those observables do not directly test currents or blocked-state time-odd observables.

The implemented density is instead

\[
\rho_{\rm inv}=\sqrt{j_\mu j^\mu}
                  =\sqrt{(j^0)^2-|\mathbf j|^2},\qquad (+---),
\]

as explicitly implemented by `invariant_baryon_density` and used to evaluate every vertex in `evaluate_rhfb_energy` ([currents.py](../dpl_rhf/rhfb/currents.py), [functional.py](../dpl_rhf/rhfb/functional.py)). This is a defensible covariant choice. Fuchs, Lenske and Wolter state that using only the time-like component of the current violates Lorentz invariance in their density-dependent hadron-field construction; their vector-density dependence uses the current invariant and produces a corresponding rearrangement field (Sec. II, Eq. (24), and Sec. II.E, Eqs. (26)–(27), PDF pp. 4–5; [PRC 52, 3043 (1995), full text](https://harvest.aps.org/v2/journals/articles/10.1103/PhysRevC.52.3043/fulltext)). That paper gives a strong theoretical precedent for an invariant-density covariant completion. It does not explicitly re-derive or refit the PKA1 parameterization with that prescription.

For a static state with \(\mathbf j=0\), \(\rho_{\rm inv}=j^0\), so the prescriptions coincide in value. For a current-carrying state, they differ: \(\partial\rho_{\rm inv}/\partial\mathbf j=-\mathbf j/\rho_{\rm inv}\), and the invariant prescription produces spatial rearrangement and changes the time-odd response. Two conclusions should be kept distinct. **Directly established:** the published DDRHF/PKA1 equations define the fitted density argument as \(j^0\); replacing it by \(\sqrt{j_\mu j^\mu}\) changes the functional away from the static rest-frame manifold. **A theoretically motivated inference:** if the goal is a Lorentz-covariant extension for current-carrying states, the invariant is the natural, and under that covariance requirement necessary, completion of a vector-density dependence. The cited sources do not say that PKA1 itself was refitted or explicitly completed this way, nor do they specify that the published j^0 convention should be carried unchanged into a fully covariant moving-state theory. Accordingly, the code can be described as a **covariant completion of the PKA1 parameterization**; its odd-state predictions remain unvalidated by the PKA1 fit or a separate physical benchmark. Once selected, this completed functional determines its time-odd fields and rearrangement by variation.

The 2022 deformed RHFB paper provides relevant but bounded support. It derives a general Bogoliubov energy with normal Hartree and Fock terms plus an anomalous pairing term (Eq. (19), PDF p. 4), and gives the PKA1 ρ-T and ρ-VT vertices and density-dependent rearrangement in the axial spherical-DWS representation (Appendix A). It also describes replacing (V) by (U) components for blocked odd neutron/proton orbits (Sec. II, PDF p. 6, around Eq. (29)). That establishes that blocking was contemplated in this model family. It does not by itself benchmark a fully unrestricted 3D time-odd current field, invariant-density rearrangement, or the implementation’s arbitrary complex active-space density against a published PKA1 odd-nucleus result. ([Geng & Long, arXiv:2202.00482](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00482), PDF pp. 4–7 and Appendix A.)

**Actionable check:** use a small, fixed basis to compare the literal published-\(j^0\) prescription with its invariant-density completion on a time-even reference, then on one blocked state with nonzero spatial current. Report separately the changes in each vertex, time-odd self-energy/rearrangement contribution, and total energy. The time-even control should coincide in the local density argument; the blocked result measures the completion’s impact. Compare with an independently implemented covariant PKA1 completion or a suitable odd-state reference if available, and label the choice so results are not mistaken for validation of the original fit.

## D1S pairing: what is supported and what remains an extension

The deformed RHFB paper explicitly adopts the finite-range Gogny D1S force in the particle-particle channel. Its Eq. (30) gives the two-Gaussian force with (W_i,B_i,H_i,M_i) and the (P_\sigma,P_\tau) exchange operators (PDF p. 6). It explicitly says that **all (J=L+S) components** are included, rather than only (J=0) (Sec. II, PDF p. 6, immediately after Eqs. (31)–(32)); Appendix B gives the detailed pairing potential (PDF pp. 25–26). The upper and lower Dirac components both occur in Eqs. (25), (31)–(32), with σ,σ′ selecting upper/lower sectors. Long et al. (2009) also use the finite-range Gogny pairing form in RHFB, Eq. (23), while their earlier deformed D-RHF comparison is described as using only (J=0); the 2022 paper is the direct precedent for all-(J) treatment ([Long et al., PRC 79, 034312 (2009), arXiv:0812.1103](https://arxiv.org/abs/0812.1103), Sec. II.B, Eq. (23), PDF p. 4; [Geng & Long 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00482), Eq. (30), PDF p. 6).

The implementation’s two-Gaussian (W,B,H,M) parameters and upper/lower Dirac spin labels are consistent with that published finite-range D1S pairing prescription ([pairing.py](../dpl_rhf/rhfb/pairing.py)). Its spherical Gaussian multipole action keeps source multipoles through the finite spinor-product bandwidth and evaluates all represented angular channels, while `GaussianPairingAction` accepts a general skew complex χ without a J=0 or time-reversal restriction ([gaussian_pairing.py](../dpl_rhf/rhfb/gaussian_pairing.py)). Thus all-J, full upper/lower-sector D1S pairing is supported in principle by the 2022 model; exact equivalence of this Cartesian contraction to the paper’s reduced equations remains to be checked.

Two scope statements need care:

* The published all-J result is derived in an **axial, reflection-symmetric DWS expansion**, using time-reversal partner notation in the Bogoliubov construction (Eqs. (15), (23)–(26)). The Cartesian code allows arbitrary complex χ and does not impose that symmetry reduction. Generalizing the kinematics is reasonable, but the reference does not certify those unrestricted matrix elements. The 2009 spherical RHFB paper’s Eq. (23) is likewise a standard spherical RHFB pairing prescription, not a benchmark for this 3D arbitrary-complex extension.
* The code accepts separate neutron and proton states only and applies D1S independently to nn and pp anomalous densities; there is no pn anomalous channel (`evaluate_rhfb_energy` requires exactly `{"n", "p"}` and creates one `GaussianPairingAction` per species). The cited 2022 paper labels odd neutron and/or proton blocking and uses (P_\tau), but the cited equations do not clearly state a general pn-pairing calculation or an explicit theorem that pn pairing is excluded. Therefore like-particle-only pairing is a conventional modeling scope and plausible match to the calculations presented, but “no pn is exactly the published same model” is too strong. In particular, its adequacy for (N\approx Z) or proton-neutron pairing regimes is not established by these papers.

**Actionable check:** first compare a tiny axial/time-reversal-invariant basis against an independent implementation of Eqs. (30)–(32) and Appendix B, checking each upper/lower block and all-(J) total, pairing energy and gap. This directly tests whether the Cartesian kernel realizes the published prescription. State that the target model is like-particle nn+pp pairing; if claims extend to systems where pn pairing may matter, add an independent pn-capable comparison before making that claim.

## Time-odd PKA1 channels and the pion Hartree term

The original PKA1 fit cites time-even ground-state and shell observables, so it cannot empirically select among time-odd extensions that coincide on its fit manifold. That limitation does **not** mean the time-odd terms of a chosen covariant Lagrangian are arbitrary. Given the PKA1 vertices and a stated density prescription, the vector, tensor, vector-tensor and pseudovector Lorentz structures determine the corresponding current channels, and the density dependence determines rearrangement by functional differentiation. The unresolved choice here is specifically the off-rest-frame density argument and its derivative, plus the absence of an external odd-state reference. The 2022 paper’s Eq. (8) lists the derivative ρ-T, symmetrized ρ-VT, and π-PV vertices (PDF pp. 3–4); Appendix A expands the axial density-dependent couplings and the derivative kernels. This supports retaining spatial components and derivative channels in the stated static Lagrangian. It does not prove the implementation’s 3D cutoff or matrix elements; the existing cutoff review correctly leaves derivative-channel angular convergence open ([PH_MULTIPOLE_CUTOFF_AUDIT_20261007.md](PH_MULTIPOLE_CUTOFF_AUDIT_20261007.md)).

The code includes a π-PV direct term from the spatial axial current in addition to its Fock term. The operator in the 2022 paper’s Eq. (8f) has spatial derivative vertices in the static reduction. The even, time-reversal-invariant reference states suppress the spatial axial-current source, so the standard PKA1 even-state results do not validate this direct Hartree term for a blocked state. Parity alone is not enough to discard it: the spatial axial current is parity-even as an axial vector, and its divergence has the pseudoscalar parity appropriate to the pion source. A nonzero time-reversal-breaking source is therefore not ruled out by parity. Conversely, the Lagrangian’s allowed vertex does not by itself establish the discrete direct contraction’s sign, normalization, or contact handling.

**Actionable check:** verify the direct π-PV source vanishes for a time-reversal-paired reference to numerical precision, then compare its sign and normalization against a direct matrix-element evaluation of Eq. (8f) for a small controlled current-carrying spinor. Keep that test distinct from the published PKA1 even-state comparison and from the separate derivative-kernel/contact convergence checks.

## Minimum claims supported by the sources

* PKA1 couplings and the ρ-T vertex are published; the fitted density argument is ρ_b (Long et al. 2007, Eqs. (8)–(11)). The invariant density is a covariant prescription with a sound precedent in Fuchs et al., but its application to PKA1 blocked/current-carrying states is an extension.
* Published deformed RHFB uses D1S in the anomalous channel and includes every J=L+S component with both upper and lower Dirac sectors (Geng & Long 2022, Eqs. (30)–(32), Appendix B). That supports the model choice, not an independently verified general-complex Cartesian implementation.
* The cited work supports neutron/proton blocking, but it does not, in the cited pairing equations, settle the full pn-pairing scope. Label the implementation’s nn+pp-only restriction explicitly.
* PKA1 Lagrangian structure motivates time-odd current terms under blocking. Published time-even fits do not validate their current-dependent density continuation or the resulting odd-state fields. A physical odd-state benchmark is still needed.
* A parity argument does not justify deleting π-PV Hartree from all time-reversal-breaking states. Its independent blocked-state matrix element still needs a reference check.
